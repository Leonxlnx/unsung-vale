# Unsung Vale: how it is built

Everything on screen is generated by code in this repository. There are no imported models
or image textures; only the audio comes from outside (see `CREDITS.md`).

## World

- **Frame**: metres, +y up. The valley runs west to east (+x). Water level is y = 0. The sun
  sits low in the east-south-east (`WORLD.sun` in `src/world/layout.js`, azimuth 33,
  elevation 12): from the Dawn Terrace it stands just to the right of the great tree. It is
  in the picture, with its glow and shafts, but off the valley's axis, so the light rakes
  across cliffs, terraces and the rings instead of leaving everything backlit. The terrain
  shadow maps are baked on the GPU at load, so the sun can be moved without `npm run world`.
- **Layout** (`src/world/layout.js`): pure data and math shared by the Node builder and the
  browser. Hand-placed mesas for the playable core (`CORE_MESAS`: dawn, petal, cantor, east,
  choir, aurel, crown ...), procedural mesas for the surrounding region, mesa-top pools.
  `makeTerrain().height(x, z)` is the analytic height function.
- **Bake** (`npm run world` -> `tools/build-world.mjs`): evaluates the height function into
  two 2048^2 maps, a 1 m core (2 km) and an 8 m region (16 km), stored as gzip'd 16-bit
  in `public/world/*.bin`. Re-run after changing the layout. Uses 4 worker threads.

## Engine (`src/engine`)

- `pipeline.js`: owns the renderer (reversed depth, 32-bit float depth), targets and the
  frame order: sky bake -> shadows -> sky + opaque scene -> copy colour and linear depth ->
  water and other transparents (`sceneT`) -> volumetrics -> fog composite -> TAA -> bloom ->
  tone map (AgX) to the canvas. `pipe.lit()` returns every uniform a lit material needs.
- `sky.js`: atmosphere (single scattering + multi-scatter fill) baked once into a warped
  panorama; volumetric clouds marched into a second panorama a few 32 px tiles per frame,
  box-filtered to remove the march dither, and cross-faded. The visible sky reads it with a
  bicubic lookup. `setCloudQuality(width, steps)` sets the panorama size (1024 in play,
  2048 on High/Ultra, 4096 for the film); `uCloudTint` warms the sunlight on the clouds.
  A small resolved copy with mips (`uSkyEnv`) serves fog colour and reflections; SH9
  ambient is computed on the CPU after each sweep. `SKY_SAMPLE`, `SH_GLSL` are the GLSL.
  Two art-direction terms sit on top of the physics: `sky.sunBoost` lifts the direct beam
  above what the baked sky implies (evening sun on level ground should be about twice the
  skylight; the bake alone gives equal parts and reads flat), and the cloud field thins
  inside a cone around the sun so the disc breaks through a gap. The sky pass adds an
  aureole around the disc.
- `lighting.js`: `shadeSurface()` (GGX + sky ambient + env reflection), `bounceLight()`
  (sunlight returned by lit cliffs, strength `uBounce`) and `sunVisibility(p)` = terrain
  shadow x object shadow x cloud shade.
- `shadows.js`: static sun shadow map for the whole core (baked once; trees enter as
  sun-facing billboards) plus two camera cascades. `shadows.addCaster(mesh, depthMat, flags)`.
  Materials compiled with `SOFT_SHADOWS` (terrain, architecture) get percentage-closer soft
  shadows: a blocker search on `uShadowRaw`, a plain-depth copy of the cascades made with
  `copyTextureToTexture`, sets the penumbra, and a receiver-plane depth bias keeps sloping
  receivers from shadowing themselves. Skinned casters copy their source's bind matrix in
  `_sync()`. `objectShadow()` has one exit on purpose: several returns inside its loop
  were miscompiled by the D3D backend.
- `volumetrics.js`: quarter-resolution ray-march of haze and mist with light shafts. The
  mist layer (`fog.mist`: density, scale height, base) fills the valley to well above the
  lake so the shafts have something to show up in; `fog.haze` is the thin air everywhere.
- `post.js`: fog composite (`FOG_GLSL`, `DEPTH_GLSL`), TAA, bloom.
- `forge.js`: `TextureForge` bakes tileable PBR layers from GLSL `material()` functions into
  array textures A (albedo + height) and B (normal xy, roughness, AO).
- `fullscreen.js`: `FullscreenPass`, `makeTarget`.

Conventions that matter:

- Scene materials are `THREE.ShaderMaterial` **without** `glslVersion` (three then defines
  `gl_FragColor`); full-screen passes are `RawShaderMaterial` with GLSL3 via `FullscreenPass`.
- Lit materials pass `defines: pipe.depthDefines` and `uniforms: { ...pipe.lit(), ... }` and
  include, in order: `COMMON, SKYMAP, SKY_SAMPLE, SH_GLSL, TERRAIN_SAMPLING, LIGHTING`.
- Loops that sample textures use `textureLod`, and textures inside branches use
  `textureGrad`, so the D3D shader compiler never has to unroll or flatten them.
- Heavy one-off GPU work (sky, terrain bakes) is always split into small scissored tiles so
  no single draw can run long on an integrated GPU.
- A fragment shader may use at most 16 textures. The terrain uses all 16, which is why the
  two occlusion maps share one array texture; adding a sampler there means merging another.
- The built-in browser pane throttles `requestAnimationFrame` when hidden; `window.__dbg`
  (`step`, `pos`, `look`, `sheet`, `gpuMs`) drives frames by hand for testing.

## World rendering (`src/world`)

- `terrain.js`: CDLOD quadtree of instanced 8x8 patches with vertex morphing. GPU-derived
  normal/curvature/cliff maps, horizon AO and sun-shadow height maps (`terrainSunVis`).
  Shading blends forge layers by slope, height and noise. Cliffs are triplanar: a far layer
  of jointed pillars and tilted blocks at every range, a close layer of fracture detail, and
  a short march through the far layer's height so the relief shadows itself. Where the real
  face is steeper than the one-metre normal map claims, the screen-space face normal takes
  over. `bakePaths(paths, clearings)` writes the worn routes and, for paved courts, a mask
  that removes ground cover and cuts the turf away under the stone.
  `terrain.heightAt(x, z)` is the CPU height query.
- `terrainMaterials.js`: GLSL for grass, soil, rock, moss, pebble, litter, snow, far layers.
  `blocks()` builds fracture cells with a squared-off distance so joints run like real ones.
- `water.js`: lake at y = 0 and pool discs; refraction, screen-space reflection, sun glitter.
- `waterfalls.js`: ballistic ribbons hugging the cliffs plus soft spray particles.
- `flora/treegen.js`: branching skeleton -> wood tubes + leaf cards per species. Each main
  bough carries one "lobe" of the crown; card normals are computed per corner from the crown
  centre and the lobe centre, so flat cards shade as one soft, billowed canopy.
  `flora/leafAtlas.js`: leaf/blossom cluster textures, two array textures: colour + coverage,
  and per-leaf data (normal tilt, thinness) so each leaf catches light on its own and glows
  when backlit. Layers: 0 maple, 1 pink blossom, 2 pale blossom, 3 conifer, 4 green leaf,
  5-8 meadow flowers, 9 wisteria racemes. `flora/forest.js`: placement, three levels of
  detail (near mesh, mid mesh, baked billboard) with dithered hand-over; `uLeafTile` repeats
  the spray inside a card (the great tree uses 4 so its leaves stay in scale).
  `flora/cliffVines.js`: ribbons of leaf sprays that start on the turf at a mesa rim and
  hang down the rock, each row pushed out to where the face actually is; kept off buildings,
  waterfalls and routes by an exclusion callback.
  `flora/grass.js`: vertex-lit grass and flowers in camera-following cells. `meadow.js` holds
  the meadow's palette and patches (lush, olive, golden, low clover) as GLSL shared by the
  blades and by the terrain under them, so gaps between blades read as more grass.
  `forest.addShrubs()` scatters low bushes through the same pipeline with one level of
  detail; `forest.addShrubAt()` plants one in an urn. `rocks.js`: instanced, plane-cut
  boulders (talus under cliffs, shore stones, meadow outcrops) with depth-only twins for
  contact shadows near the camera.
- `arch/builder.js`: `MeshBuilder` (boxes, lathes, arches) with per-vertex material ids.
  `arch/kit.js`: lancet, tower, hall, pavilion, terrace, arcade, viaduct, citadel, the
  tuning spire and the stepped cliff quarters.
  `arch/archMaterial.js`: stone/gold/tile/glass layers and the triplanar shader. Floors
  inside a court listed in `uPlazas` (`sites.plazas`: centre, radius, points) carry an
  inlaid rose of polished marble and banded borders; all paving gets drifts of fallen petals.
  `arch/floorGloss.js`: screen-space reflections for those floors, drawn as additive sheets
  in `sceneT` (one jittered mirror ray per pixel, blended into a soft reflection by TAA;
  setting `reflections`, on for High, Ultra and the film).
  `arch/sites.js`: where things are built.
- `boats.js`: a handful of white-and-gold barges on the lake, lofted from quads.
- `landmarks.js`: the Lattice rings. `dressing.js`: banners, wisteria, drifting petals,
  butterflies over the meadows, birds, and dust motes that blaze when the sun is behind
  them (additive, in `sceneT`).

## Performance targets

Developed on an AMD Radeon 780M (integrated). Budget is roughly 16 ms at 1080p with the
render scale around 0.65 and TAA. Measured there with nothing else running (1080p window):
Low about 60 fps, Medium about 52, High about 45, Ultra about 39. About half of a Medium
frame is fixed post-processing at display resolution (anti-aliasing, bloom, tone map), the
rest the scene. Shadows are the largest single cost, so Low and Medium redraw the two
cascades on alternate frames (`shadows.rate = 2`) and Medium keeps soft penumbrae to the
near cascade (`shadows.soft = 'near'`). Measure with `node tools/perf.mjs` and `node tools/profile.mjs` on an
idle machine only: other GPU work makes the numbers meaningless. `node tools/fps.mjs` lets
the page run freely in a window and counts presented frames (the rate a player sees).

`engine/governor.js` holds the frame rate chosen in the settings (`targetFps`, 50 by
default, 0 = off): it counts the frames actually shown over short windows and moves the
render scale in steps of 0.05 between 0.55 and the preset's own scale, looking for the
highest scale that still holds the target (it remembers for a while where it had to back off). A change of scale keeps the anti-aliasing history and the bloom
chain (`pipe.setSize(w, h, true)`), so it passes without a flicker. The sky is drawn after
the opaque scene at the far plane with the depth test on, so it is only shaded where it shows.

## Game (`src/game`, `src/ui`)

- `game.js`: rules and flow (`home`, `playing`, `paused`, `ending`). Owns `Player`, `CameraRig`,
  `CollisionWorld`, `Particles`, `Enemies`, the three spires, the light bridge and saving
  (`localStorage`). Emits events (`state`, `objective`, `boss`, `tuned`, `player`, `enemy`,
  `hit`, `ending`) that the UI and audio subscribe to with `game.on(fn)`.
- `player.js`: kinematic capsule; run, sprint, jump, roll, three-step combo, heavy strike,
  resonance pulse. Facing convention: yaw 0 looks along +z, forward = (sin yaw, 0, cos yaw).
- `collision.js`: terrain height field + three-mesh-bvh over the playable architecture and
  the boulders (each rock cell becomes one geometry the first time the player comes near:
  `world.addLazy` / `world.stream`), and capsules for tree trunks in a spatial grid
  (`forest.trunkColliders`). `resolveCapsule` pushes a character out of all of them, and
  `raycast` (the camera) stops at them. `game._bodies` keeps hero and constructs apart.
- `input.js`: pointer lock with `unadjustedMovement` (raw mouse counts, no pointer
  acceleration); rogue jumps in the reported movement are dropped.
- `story.js`: the 21 s opening (`game.state === 'intro'`: it leads the camera and has the
  hero kneel and rise), lines of narration (`say(id, text)`, each once per journey), and the
  current goal. `game.marker` is where the HUD marker points: breadcrumbs along `LEGS`
  until the site is near, so the goal is always a short walk ahead.
- `notes.js`: lost notes set down every 46 m along the same paths; gathering one mends a
  little health and sounds the next step of a scale.
- `enemies.js`: the Mute (thrall, lancer, colossus). Each body is a set of instanced crystal
  shards moved toward formation slots; they lie dormant as rubble until approached.
  They move through `tryMove`: follow the floor, get pushed aside by walls, shafts and trunks
  (`world.resolveCapsule`), and go round to one side when the straight line is blocked.
- Spires are tuned (hold E) once their guards are gone. Untuned spires project a "mute zone"
  (`pipe.fog.mute[i]`) that the fog pass renders as drained colour; tuning collapses it.
- `particles.js`: pooled additive sparks, embers and glows, stretched along their velocity.
  `vfx.js`: effects that need the finished frame or real geometry, drawn in `sceneT`:
  `wave()` is a shockwave (a ring over the ground or a bubble seen from any side) that bends
  the picture behind it by sampling `uOpaque`; `helix(spire)` is the strands of light that
  climb a spire when it is tuned. The fog pass spares very bright pixels from the mute
  zones' duotone, so this light keeps its colour inside the silence. `game.flash` lifts the
  exposure for a breath on the big moments, and `uLattice` (architecture material) lets the
  great rings glow for every spire restored.
- `strings.js`: strings of light between pylons at the courts. A link is awake when the
  spires it names are tuned; `route(from, to)` finds the hops over awake links and
  `start(hops)` carries the hero along them (player state `ride`: the string moves him,
  the hero hangs from the blade laid across it). `strings.goal` is the court the story
  wants, so the pylon's prompt offers the whole way there.
- `encounters.js`: who stands where. `WAVES` per outer court (the next wave rises when the
  last is down and the hero is still there), elites as modifiers of a kind (`derive` in
  `enemies.js`: scale, health, damage, an ember tint through the third instance channel),
  packs in the wilds (`wildN` groups, remembered in `game.wildDone`). `cleared(id)` is what
  lets a spire be tuned. Bosses with `phases` change at health thresholds: a roar that
  summons others (event `summon`), then rings of blast marks (attack kind 3).
  Constructs further than 240 m from the hero are neither updated nor drawn.
- `bells.js`: three bells on each outer mesa, each with a guard (`bell:<court>:<i>` groups).
  `bells.done(id)` gates the court: its waves are only called up (`encounters.callCourt`)
  when the third has rung, and `cleared(id)` needs it too.
- `people.js`: the people of the Vale, built from turned shapes and shaded with the hero's
  material. Each has its own materials so it can go from stone to dyed cloth while waking
  (`_dye`). `speak(n, lines, done)` runs a conversation: an over-the-shoulder camera, lines
  advanced with E, events `dialogue` for the interface.
- `quests.js`: side quests as data (`QUESTS`: gather things at spots, clear packs, find
  notes), what each person says (`talkTo`), and the gathered things themselves.
  `trials.js`: rings on the mesas that start a wave fight or a timed run through gates.
  `game.tracker()` is the side goal the HUD shows; `game.grant({ up, xp })` gives a reward.
- Growth: `SKILLS` in `game.js` maps each spire to an ability (`player.skills`),
  `player.up` holds steps of life, breath and edge (`healthMax`, `staminaMax`, `power`),
  `game.buy(kind)` is the luthier's bench (resonance, `game.xp`, earned from every construct).
- `game.openMenu(name)` puts a screen over the game (state `menu`: map, journal, bench).
- `settings.js`: quality presets and options. `ui/ui.js` + `ui/ui.css`: all interface: HUD
  (compass, goal and tracker, markers, vitals as strings), conversation, the journey screens.
  The map is drawn once from `terrain.heightAt` into a canvas (`_bakeMap`) and overlaid
  every frame while open (`_drawMap`).
- `main.js`: boot sequence with staged loading, the title camera drift, and `window.__dbg`.
## Hero, audio and film

- `anim/rig.js`: `Rig` (poses as character-space rotations relative to rest; bone names are
  matched with punctuation stripped, so `UpperArm.L` and `UpperArmL` are the same) and
  `SpringChains` (verlet cape and tabard bones with body collision).
- `anim/hero.js`: loads `public/models/hero.glb` (built by `tools/blender/hero.py`), swaps in
  the game's materials by glTF material name (Armor, Gold, Cloth, Cape, Leather, Mask, Blade,
  Undersuit), and animates procedurally from `Player` state. `hero.debugPose` is a hook for
  trying pose offsets from the console.
  Legs and the sword arm are solved by inverse kinematics (`rig.ik`, two bones toward a
  target with a pole; `rig.setCharQ` holds a foot flat whatever the leg does). Moving, the
  feet follow a gait cycle whose stride and timing come from the ground speed, so they do not
  slide; standing or fighting, each foot stays planted in the world until the body has moved
  away from it and then takes a step. The sword is described in the chest's own space
  (`SW`: where the hand grips, where the blade points), so turning the body swings the
  blade. Each strike is a short table of keys in `ATTACKS` (sword, hips, chest, pelvis, feet);
  the trail behind the blade is sampled from that table between frames, so the arc is smooth
  at any frame rate. Footfalls are emitted by the feet themselves (`player` event `step`).
  `__dbg.shots({ press: ['attack'] })` shows a grid of frames through the game's camera.
- `audio/audio.js` + `audio/director.js`: Web Audio buses, music cross-fades, ambience by
  location, positional one-shots; everything comes from `public/audio/manifest.json`.
  Effects are layered in the director (a hit is steel + a chip of crystal + weight on the
  heavy ones), never repeat the same variation twice running, can be delayed to land on the
  cut, and feed a convolution reverb (`ir/space.ogg`, a recorded stone court) through their
  own send. `audio.hold()` plays a sustained tone for as long as something is held.
- `cine/director.js`: the 45 s film as a list of shots (`setup`, `update`), driving the hero
  through `input.puppet` and the story state through `game.cineSetup()`.
  Each shot can pass a lens to `d.cam()`: focus distance and blur.
  `cine/output.js`: depth of field (a gather blur per sub-frame), sub-frame accumulation
  (motion blur), HDR10 (PQ, BT.2020) and SDR output.
  `tools/capture.mjs` receives frames over HTTP and pipes them into ffmpeg.
  After any raw GL call use `pipe.resetGL()`, never `renderer.resetState()` alone: three's
  reset turns the reversed depth buffer off.

## Checks that need no rendering

Most of the play feel was tuned by measuring rather than by eye, with the page held
(`sessionStorage.uvHold = '1'`) and `game.update(1 / 60)` called in a loop from the console:
foot speed while planted (it should be zero), knee and wrist angles through every move, the
largest jump of any joint between two frames (pose pops), the capsule's step-by-step motion
on stairs, camera jerk, whether every construct reaches a player who stands still, how many
strikes of a combo land from a given distance, and a fuzz run of random input at twenty places
looking for stuck spots, falls through the world and NaNs. `__dbg.bot()` plays the whole game.

## Development helpers

`window.__dbg` in the page: `step(n)`, `pos/look` (free camera), `play(frames, moveY, moveX,
{ tp, press, hold })` (scripted play), `sheet()` (four test views in one image), `crop(x, y,
w, h)` (magnify canvas pixels), `strip({...})` (the hero filmed from the side as a strip of
snapshots, for judging animation), `cine(t)` (jump the film to a time), `reel(times)` (a
contact sheet of the film), `filmStill(t)` (one graded film frame with depth of field) and
`gpuMs()`. `bot(seconds)` plays the inner Vale in film mode without drawing. `botPlay(seconds,
{ fresh: true })` plays the whole game under the real rules (it follows the story's goals,
talks to people, does their quests, begins trials, rides the strings and strikes the notes
of each spire); call it in chunks until it reports `ended`, and read `time`, `quests` and
`log` in what it returns. A full run is about 43 simulated minutes. `node tools/tour.mjs`
opens a real Chrome window, sets up one situation after another (talk, map, ride, waves,
elite, boss, trial, bell, swimming, tuning) and saves a screenshot of each to
`tools/out/tour/`: the quickest way to look at everything. Call `pipe.setRenderScale(1)` before judging detail: the Medium preset renders at
two thirds scale. `tools/profile.mjs` times a frame with parts of the scene switched off.
`?perf=1` benchmarks the presets (`tools/perf.mjs`),
`?capture=1...` is used by the film capture. Editing files under `src/` while a capture is
running makes Vite reload the page and ruins the render.

Setting `sessionStorage.uvHold = '1'` in a dev page stops it drawing on its own, so frames are
only rendered when stepped through `__dbg`; useful when the GPU is needed elsewhere.
To try a sun position without reloading: `pipe.sky.setSun(az, el); terrain.queueBakes();
pipe.shadows._staticDirty = true`, then `terrain.bakeStep(8)` until it returns 0.
